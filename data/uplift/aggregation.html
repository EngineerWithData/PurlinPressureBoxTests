SteelData Initiative · Module 5 · Aggregation

MBMA Full-Scale C- and Z-Purlin Roof System Test Database (1976-1997)

Eleven MBMA full-scale purlin test reports were pooled into five comparable populations defined by loading direction, panel attachment (through-fastened versus standing seam) and span continuity, with each paper's Table S1 strength summary as the primary table and its dimension, coupon and prediction tables joined as lookups. Rows were pooled only where the member type, panel restraint and span condition match, so MBMA_8404 splits across an uplift and a gravity group and the fastener-pullover program stands alone.

4 COMPARABLE GROUPS · 199 SPECIMENS

Rows may be pooled only when they describe the same kind of test on the same kind of member. Each group below is one such population; specimens are never matched between papers, they are pooled as independent samples.

Simple-span C and Z purlins with through-fastened panels under uplift

116 specimens

Simple-span (single-span) assemblies of two C or Z purlin lines with a through-fastened (screwed) roof panel on the tension flange, loaded to failure by chamber suction; all rows share the same member type, panel attachment and span condition, so their strengths are directly comparable. Includes the MBMA_1103 wall girt tests (same test arrangement).

How a row was built

Each paper's tables are joined into specimen rows on the specimen label, values stated once in a table note are filled in for every row of that table, and values absent as columns are reconstructed from the nondimensional ratios the papers tabulate. A reported value is never overwritten by a derived one.

This run derived 138 values and cross-checked 102 that were both reported and derivable.

Aggregation, not matching

Specimens are pooled as independent samples. One paper's specimen Z1 has nothing to do with another's Z1, so nothing is joined across papers. What is compared across papers is the distribution of each variable, not any individual test.

Read the overlap before the mean

A pooled mean over papers that explored different parameter ranges describes the mixture, not the physics. The overlap column says how much of each variable's range all the papers share: where it is low, the pooled statistics are dominated by whichever paper reached that far.

Judgement calls to review

  • The central split is loading direction and panel attachment: uplift puts the unattached (compression) flange free to move, gravity does not, and standing seam clips give far less rotational restraint than screwed panels. I therefore kept four purlin groups (simple-span through-fastened uplift, standing seam uplift, continuous through-fastened uplift, simple-span gravity) rather than pooling by section shape. C and Z sections are pooled within each group because section_type is carried as an identifier column and every member reports it.
  • MBMA_8404 contributes to two groups (its uplift and its gravity rows), split by a row filter on load_direction.
  • The simulated-gravity group (MBMA_8404 only, 7 rows) and the fastener-pullover group (MBMA_9410 only, 12 rows) each come from a single paper. They are retained because they are the only data of their kind in this collection, as permitted; a reviewer may drop them if single-source groups are unwanted.
  • MBMA_9410's standard pullover coupon tests (Tables 3-1 to 3-9) and simulated component tests (Tables 4-1 to 4-8) are deliberately excluded: they load one screw directly and are not full-scale assembly tests, so they are not comparable with the vacuum-box rows.
  • Reported failure loads are not consistently dead-load corrected: MBMA_8703/8803 subtract the assembly dead weight, MBMA_8404 gravity adds it, MBMA_7603/8310/8702/9001/9105 report applied chamber load only. Any cross-paper strength comparison should carry this flag.
