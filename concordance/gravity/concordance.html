Summary Table of Common Measured Variables

Vacuum-chamber tests of cold-formed C and Z purlin roofs. All nine MBMA reports are full-scale vacuum-chamber load tests of cold-formed C- and Z-purlin roof assemblies, reporting a measured failure load (or moment), the observed failure mode, the measured purlin geometry and coupon yield stress, and a comparison with an AISI or base-test predicted capacity. They diverge in span arrangement (single-span base tests vs two- and three-span lapped systems), panel system (through-fastened vs standing seam with various clips and insulation), load direction (gravity in most, gravity plus uplift in MBMA_8404), and in how strength is normalised — line load in plf, moment in kip-in., or pressure in psf — while MBMA_7506 alone reports instrument-level strain, deflection and rotation histories.

How a row is built

Every quantity in every extracted table is inventoried under the exact column name the paper printed, one paper at a time. Those inventories are then clustered by physical meaning, so the same quantity under three different symbols becomes one row. Notation is normalised only at the end, never before the clustering.

Units

One canonical unit per row, the one most common across the sources, with its SI equivalence beside it. A paper reporting in another unit keeps its own value in its CSV and carries a conversion chip here, so the step is visible and reversible.

Availability

The fraction counts every paper where the variable can be obtained at all. The bar splits that into directly reported and qualified, because a row that is fully available but mostly implied is weaker than one that is fully direct, and a single percentage hides it.

Judgement calls to review

  • Failure load is canonicalized as plf per purlin line, but MBMA_7506 reports it as a uniform pressure in psf; a tributary-width conversion is needed and the tributary width differs between papers (and is explicitly questioned in MBMA_9704, where 3.75 ft is used despite ~5 ft spacing).
  • Papers differ on whether the reported failure load includes dead load: MBMA_8404, MBMA_9002, MBMA_9704 and MBMA_9906 include self weight, MBMA_9804 reports applied load separately from total load, and MBMA_8309/MBMA_7506 do not state it.
  • Chamber pressure at failure was merged across psf (MBMA_9704) and inches of water (MBMA_9804, MBMA_9906); MBMA_7506's per-increment manometer load was placed here too although it is a load history rather than a single failure value — reviewers may prefer a separate row.
  • Test-to-predicted ratio merges dimensionless ratios (MBMA_8309, 8404, 8903, 9804, 9906 Rt) with percentages (MBMA_9704 percent_of_predicted, MBMA_9002 R%). The denominators differ substantially (AISI 1980, AISI 1986, base-test method, shear+bending), so the row should not be pooled statistically without noting the method.
  • Computed flexural capacity merges allowable-based values multiplied by a safety factor (MBMA_8309 M1/M2, MBMA_8903 maisi, MBMA_8404 m_u) with nominal SeFy values (MBMA_9002 Mn, MBMA_9804 SeFy, MBMA_9906 Mnt); units include in-kip and ft-kips.
  • Measured section thickness and tensile coupon thickness were deliberately kept as separate rows, since several papers report both and they differ.
  • Effective section modulus (post-buckling, AISI) was kept separate from gross section moduli; MBMA_8404's s_e_in3 is actually an effective flange width despite its heading and was not placed in either row.
  • Flange width, lip length, lip angle and corner radii rows each merge top/bottom (or B/B1) columns of the same paper into one canonical row; per-flange resolution is preserved only in the symbol field.
  • MBMA_9610 supplied no inventory content and therefore appears in no row.
  • MBMA_9804's 'r' (base test reduction factor) is labelled a material_property in the source but was placed under derived_ratio as it is a system factor, not a steel property.
  • MBMA_8309 removed by hand 2026-11-10 (Cris Moen): EXCLUDED 2026-11-10 (Cris Moen): edge-stiffener study on a narrow two-purlin specimen (purlins 18 in. apart under a 34 in. panel, Fig. 3 p.11); failure moments only, panel/fastening/bracing not described; not a roof-system test
