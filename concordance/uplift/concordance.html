Summary Table of Common Measured Variables

Full-scale uplift tests of C and Z purlins and girts. All seventeen MBMA reports document full-scale vacuum-chamber tests of cold-formed C and Z purlin or girt assemblies with attached metal panels, reporting a failure load, a failure mode, the measured cross-section geometry and the coupon yield stress, and most compare the result with a code or analytical prediction. They diverge in how the failure load is expressed (chamber pressure, line load per purlin line, moment or axial load), in span continuity and bracing, and in scope: several add panel-to-purlin rotational restraint F-tests, strain and deflection histories, standing-seam base-test predictions, or screw pullover component tests.

How a row is built

Every quantity in every extracted table is inventoried under the exact column name the paper printed, one paper at a time. Those inventories are then clustered by physical meaning, so the same quantity under three different symbols becomes one row. Notation is normalised only at the end, never before the clustering.

Units

One canonical unit per row, the one most common across the sources, with its SI equivalence beside it. A paper reporting in another unit keeps its own value in its CSV and carries a conversion chip here, so the step is visible and reversible.

Availability

The fraction counts every paper where the variable can be obtained at all. The bar splits that into directly reported and qualified, because a row that is fully available but mostly implied is weaker than one that is fully direct, and a single percentage hides it.

Judgement calls to review

  • Measured failure loads are reported in three different physical forms across the set: chamber pressure (psf or psi), line load per purlin line (plf), and for MBMA_9003 an axial load in kips. I kept 'Failure line load per purlin line' and 'Chamber pressure at failure' as separate rows, and placed MBMA_9003's mixed kips/plf column in the line-load row with a unit caveat; a reviewer should decide whether the axial-load tests belong in that row at all.
  • MBMA_7501's pilot screw-connection rows report load per screw under the same failure_load_as_printed column as the psf assembly loads; I listed that paper in both the pressure row and the fastener-load row.
  • Tributary width is printed in some reports, derived by the curator in MBMA_7603 and MBMA_1103, and appears only inside a conversion formula in MBMA_8310 (38 in.). Treated as one row despite differing availability.
  • Panel-to-member rotational restraint is reported with incompatible units: kip-in/in/rad (MBMA_8001, MBMA_8703), lb/in/in (MBMA_8310) and N/mm slopes (MBMA_1103). These are dimensionally related but not identical; conversion must be checked before pooling.
  • MBMA_1103's Table 5/C column 'thickness_in' is insulation thickness, not steel thickness. It is kept in the Insulation row, not in Base metal thickness.
  • Measured section thickness and coupon thickness are merged into one 'Base metal thickness' row for most papers, but MBMA_8803 and MBMA_9105 print coupon thickness as a separate quantity from the purlin micrometer thickness; only the purlin value was assigned to that row.
  • Test-to-predicted ratios were pooled into one row even though the denominators differ by method (Cornell, AISI drafts, MBMA proposal, base test method) and MBMA_9702 inverts the sense (predicted/experimental). Rows should be filtered by method before any statistics.
  • MBMA_1103's R-factor (Mt/SeFy) and MBMA_9105's Mu/Mth are the same AISI reduction factor R and were merged into the test-to-predicted ratio row; a reviewer may prefer a dedicated 'reduction factor R' row.
  • Gross/effective moments of inertia, section moduli and program-computed section properties were grouped into one specimen-parameter row; effective section modulus Se was kept separate because it is the key strength input.
  • MBMA_8001 reports corner radii only as radius/thickness ratios while other papers report absolute radii; they are in the same row with a unit caveat.
  • MBMA_9410 is a fastener-pullover study rather than a purlin-bending study, so most of its rows (fastener load, rib profile, transverse stress-strain model) do not overlap with the purlin papers; only load direction, span, spans/lines, panel type and coupon properties overlap.
  • MBMA_9001, MBMA_9501 and MBMA_9902 supplied no variable inventories and therefore appear in no member list.
